FAQ

Generator questions, answered

How big a generator do I need?
For a typical 2,500 sq ft Austin home with central AC, gas heat, and standard appliances, a 14kW–18kW unit usually runs everything. Larger homes (4,000+ sq ft), homes with electric water heaters, or homes with two HVAC zones often need 22kW–26kW. We do a real load calculation rather than guessing — under-sizing leads to nuisance trips during outages.
What's included in installation?
Concrete or composite pad, generator unit, automatic transfer switch (ATS), gas line connection (we partner with a licensed plumber), all electrical wiring, weatherproofing, permits, inspection, load testing, and a walkthrough of operation and the monitoring app.
Do you handle the gas line?
Yes. We partner with a licensed plumber for the gas line — included in our quote. If you don't have natural gas, we also install propane systems with above-ground or underground tanks (250–500 gal recommended for whole-home backup).
How loud is it?
Modern Generac and Kohler standby units are around 65–70 dB at 23 feet — about the volume of a normal conversation or a window AC unit. Quieter than a lawnmower. We also coordinate placement to minimize noise toward bedrooms and neighbors.
Will my HOA allow it?
Most Austin-area HOAs allow standby generators with reasonable placement and screening. We've installed in HOAs across Westlake, Lakeway, Round Rock, and Cedar Park. We help with HOA paperwork and provide manufacturer specs as needed.
What's the maintenance requirement?
Annual service to maintain warranty. We offer a $229/year maintenance plan that covers everything. Without service, manufacturers can deny warranty claims.
Do you offer financing?
Yes. 0% for 12 months, or extended terms up to 84 months at competitive rates. See financing options →
Can I use a portable generator instead?
Portables work for short outages and a few essentials, but they require manual fueling (4–6 gallons per 8 hours), can't run AC reliably, and create CO risk. For Austin's pattern of long-duration outages during ice storms or heat waves, a standby is the only realistic solution for whole-home backup.
